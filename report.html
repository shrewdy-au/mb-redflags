Team MOGL: hackathon report

Microburbs hackathon, 7 Nov 2026. Team MOGL (Jake Milne). Entries: Task 1 (social post: two short documentary reels) and Task 3 (Chrome extension: MB Red Flags). Decision support and journalism, not financial or legal advice.

Task 1: social posts

Reel 1 (/demo.mp4, pitch /pitch.mp4): "Waterloo: 749 public homes out, 3,000 in". Reel 2 (/task1a/demo.mp4): "Zetland: Randwick objected, the City backed it".

Data

  • Microburbs hackathon API (City of Sydney LGA): suburb tenure (demographics/tenure), public-housing layers (risks/all, surroundings/public-housing), suburb listings and properties.
  • Reel 2 also uses Microburbs' national API for Little Bay (outside the hackathon area), licensed to MOGL.
  • Maps: OpenFreeMap tiles from OpenStreetMap data (© OpenStreetMap contributors) with the OpenMapTiles schema; terrain from Geoscience Australia.
  • On-screen claims: ABC News, NSW Planning Portal, The Urban Developer. Every figure is listed with its source in the README.

Method

  1. Pick one planning story per suburb where the API can show the "before" on a map: who lives there now (tenure share, public-housing layer) and what is planned.
  2. Write a 60 to 90 second script where every number has a named source; render the map shots from the API layers on open map tiles.
  3. Voice, grade and captions; no call to action.

Held-out check (reels)

The headline figures (749 public homes to be replaced; about 3,300 new homes on the estate, rounded to 3,000 in the title) were cross-checked against the ABC News article and against the API: demographics/tenure gives Waterloo (NSW) a 2021 Census public-housing share of 20.2%, consistent with a large existing estate. The API does not count individual dwellings to be demolished, so 749 rests on the published source, not the API.

What worked / what didn't

  • Worked: the public-housing layer maps cleanly onto the estate boundary, so the "before" is visible rather than asserted.
  • Didn't: the hackathon API stops at the City of Sydney boundary, so the Little Bay half of the Zetland story needed MOGL's licensed national API.

Task 3: MB Red Flags (Chrome extension)

When you open a realestate.com.au or domain.com.au listing in the City of Sydney, a side panel gives one verdict (GO / INVESTIGATE / NO-GO) and the rows behind it, each labelled with the level its fact applies at (property, 200 m, mesh block, SA1, street, suburb, agency) and the distance where there is one.

Data

  • Microburbs hackathon API: geocode/address, properties/{gnaf}/profile, risks/all, surroundings/public-housing, street/profile, valuation/avm, valuation/agent-quoting, transport/nearby, suburbs/{SAL}/risks/extras, suburbs/{SAL}/risks/property-hazard-counts. Scripts only: suburbs/{SAL}/listings/for-sale, suburbs/{SAL}/properties, suburbs/{SAL}/demographics/tenure.
  • From the listing page: the address only. The asking price is typed in by the user.
  • No API output, keys or CSV rows are stored in the repo.

Method

  1. Resolve the address to a G-NAF id: the geocode candidate whose abbr matches street number and street name, with a bonus for the unit (UNIT 3/12 …) and the suburb, and a penalty when a house address would land on a unit.
  2. 8 calls with Promise.allSettled, at most 4 at a time, through a local proxy that holds the key (8 req/s token bucket, honours 429 Retry-After; 403 = outside coverage; 404 and data:null = no data, shown grey).
  3. Hazards from risks/all: on-property row first, then the nearest, with a buffer per hazard.
  4. Public housing at two scales: the SA1 share of the area the address sits in (the layer lists only areas with public housing, so "not in the layer" reads as none) and the 200 m flagged share, red if the address itself is flagged.
  5. Aircraft noise: profile.mesh_block joined to risks/extras.per_mb.risks[].anef_med, falling back to suburb property-hazard-counts.aircraft_noise; shown grey when neither has data.
  6. Street forecast rank vs the suburb median; asking price vs AVM; agency quoting behaviour.
  7. Any RED → NO-GO, any AMBER → INVESTIGATE, otherwise GO. Neutral wording: what to check, not what to buy.

Held-out check (extension)

scripts/holdout-sa1.mjs: 5 suburbs (Redfern, Waterloo, Surry Hills, Alexandria, Zetland), first 10 addresses each from suburbs/{SAL}/properties. The extension's area flag (SA1 public-housing share ≥ 10%) is compared with (A) the 2021 Census mesh-block share from demographics/tenure ≥ 10% and (B) the 200 m flagged share from surroundings/public-housing ≥ 5% or the address itself flagged. Reading: the shares move together (Pearson 0.905 and 0.842) and when the area flag fires it is usually right (precision 0.75), but it misses most mesh blocks above 10% (recall 0.214). An SA1 averages over several mesh blocks, so a concentrated estate block is diluted, and addresses outside the layer count as 0%. That is why the panel also shows the 200 m row and the nearest area at or above 20%. Caveats: the sample is the API's first 10 addresses per suburb, not random; 10 addresses had no tenure row for their mesh block. If the supplied CSVs are available, scripts/holdout.mjs runs the CSV-based check as well.

What worked / what didn't

  • Worked: the full pipeline ran live on real listings, houses and units (UNIT 111/1A … forms), in about a second per address; outside-coverage addresses make no calls.
  • Worked: every row carries its level and distance, so a suburb fact never reads as a property fact.
  • Didn't: no suburb we tested returns anef_med in risks/extras or a value in aircraft_noise, so the ANEF row is grey in live mode.
  • Didn't: the heritage layer is on-property for most inner-city terraces, so almost every listing gets at least one amber.
  • Didn't: public_housing in risks/all returns up to 20 nearby areas, not the two rows the docs suggested; we now pick the on-property row and the nearest.
  • Didn't: the API has no portal URLs, so the demo listings were matched to portal pages by hand.

What buyers already see

Listing pages show the price guide (when given), photos, floor plan, suburb median and schools. Domain's property profiles flag flood, bushfire and heritage as detected or not. Neither portal shows public-housing share, aircraft noise or agent quoting behaviour.
